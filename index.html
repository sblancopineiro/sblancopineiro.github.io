I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the Institut d'Anàlisi Econòmica of the Consejo Superior de Investigaciones Científicas (IAE-CSIC) and an affiliated researcher at the Institut d'Economia de Barcelona (IEB). Previously I worked as a research assistant at the Universidad Carlos III de Madrid and at the IAE-CSIC, collaborating on projects in labor economics, gender economics and migration.

My research interests lie in labor and family economics, with a particular focus on causal inference and applied econometrics. I work mainly with large administrative microdata to evaluate how public policies shape family formation, dissolution and labor-market outcomes.

Publications

Improving Social Inclusion of Female Migrants: A Randomised Controlled Intervention in Spain

with Antonio Cabrales and Warn N. Lekfuangfu

SERIEs, Journal of the Spanish Economic Association, 2026.

Abstract

This study evaluates the impact of a randomised controlled intervention that provided psychosocial support and digital skills training to socially excluded female migrants living in Murcia, Spain, all of whom were also beneficiaries of the Minimum Basic Income Scheme. The programme significantly improves participants' knowledge of community resources, use of social support networks, digital skills and mental well-being. We find no effects on employment or labour-market participation, based on both survey responses and administrative records. Non-financial interventions can therefore enhance social inclusion and personal well-being even when they do not improve labour-market outcomes in the short run.
